# The Hero Trap

Early-stage ventures almost always begin with heroes. A single engineer works an 80-hour week to save the database; a charismatic founder closes every deal through sheer personal willpower.

While heroic effort is necessary to break inertia at day zero, it becomes the **single greatest bottleneck to scale** at day 100. When an organization depends on heroism:
- Knowledge is trapped in individual skulls.
- Vacation becomes an existential operational risk.
- New hires take six months to make their first safe commit.
- Quality fluctuates wildly based on daily caffeine levels.

To build an enduring company, you must deliberately transition from **heroic execution** to **systemic execution**.

---

## 1. Documentation as Compiled Architecture

Too many teams treat documentation as a chore done after the fact—an outdated graveyard of Confluence pages that nobody reads.

In high-performing operational cultures, documentation is treated like source code:
- **It is versioned and owned.**
- **It defines the runtime protocol.**
- **If a process is undocumented, it does not exist.**

When an operator documents a standard operating procedure (SOP) or a systems builder writes a crisp specification document (PRD), they are essentially writing high-level code for human agents to execute deterministically.

---

## 2. Decision Rights: DACI Over Consensus

Consensus is the enemy of velocity. When decisions require universal agreement:
- Bold moves get diluted down to inoffensive mediocrity.
- Accountability evaporates into committee mist.
- Deadlines slip as debates drag on endlessly.

Instead, enforce the **DACI Framework**:
- **Driver:** The one person responsible for driving the decision to completion.
- **Approver:** The sole person who holds final veto power.
- **Contributors:** Domain experts consulted for technical or business context.
- **Informed:** Stakeholders notified of the outcome after the decision is made.

With DACI, everyone knows who has the baton. Disagreement is welcomed, but once the Driver and Approver decide, everyone commits.

---

## 3. The Minimum Viable Process

Process is not bureaucracy unless it exists without purpose.
> *The optimal amount of process is the minimum viable structure that prevents catastrophic errors while enabling maximum team velocity.*

Build systems that make the right behavior the path of least resistance.
